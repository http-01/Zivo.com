Taylor Swift

Singer-Songwriter

01

Overview

A songwriter who moved her family to Nashville as a teenager chasing country radio, and went on to redefine what a modern pop career looks like.

02

Early Life

Taylor Alison Swift grew up on a Christmas tree farm near Wyomissing, Pennsylvania, the daughter of a former stockbroker and a former marketing executive. Her maternal grandmother had been a professional opera singer, and Swift has traced her own early interest in performing back to watching her.

She performed at local fairs and singing competitions as a child, sang the national anthem at a Philadelphia 76ers game at eleven, and began writing her own songs and learning guitar at twelve.

03

Education

To be closer to Nashville's country music industry, Swift's family relocated from Pennsylvania to Hendersonville, Tennessee, when she was thirteen, and she completed her schooling there while pursuing songwriting and recording opportunities.

04

Career

Swift signed a songwriting deal with Sony/ATV at fourteen, becoming the company's youngest-ever songwriter, and released her self-titled debut country album in 2006. Successive albums moved her steadily from country into pop, with 1989 (2014) marking a full genre transition and Reputation, Lover, Folklore, and Evermore each expanding her range further.

In 2019, a dispute over ownership of her early masters led Swift to begin re-recording her first six albums as “Taylor's Version,” a project widely credited with reshaping conversations around artists' rights in the music industry. Her Eras Tour (2023–2024) became the highest-grossing concert tour in history, and she has continued releasing new music, including 2025's The Life of a Showgirl.

05

Achievements

  • Winner of multiple Grammy Awards across country, pop, and album of the year categories
  • The Eras Tour became the highest-grossing concert tour of all time, earning roughly $2 billion
  • Re-recorded and reclaimed control of her early catalog through the “Taylor's Version” project
  • Youngest songwriter ever signed by Sony/ATV Music Publishing, at age 14
  • One of the best-selling recording artists in history across physical, digital, and streaming formats
06

Interesting Facts

  • Was named after singer-songwriter James Taylor
  • Wrote her first song, “Lucky You,” at age twelve
  • Was turned down by every major label in Nashville before signing with independent label Big Machine Records
  • Has a younger brother, Austin Swift, who works as an actor and producer
  • Voiced a supporting character in the 2012 animated film The Lorax
